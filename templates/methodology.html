Methodology

How the hotspot scores are built

Every place gets a 0 to 100 gender hotspot score for water, energy, and agriculture. This page explains what goes into the score, how to read it, and where the data comes from.

The score Risk classes Sector indicators Data status Limits

The score

Each sector score combines three components, each on a 0 to 1 scale. Exposure and sensitivity raise the score. Adaptive capacity lowers it, so it enters as its gap from full capacity.

Gender hotspot score (Exposure + Sensitivity + (1 − Adaptive capacity)) ÷ 3 × 100
Vulnerability score (0.40 × Exposure + 0.35 × Sensitivity + 0.25 × (1 − Adaptive capacity)) × 100

The composite score of a place is the average of its three sector hotspot scores. The sector with the highest score is shown as the place's top pressure.

E

Exposure

How strongly the place is exposed to climate hazards such as drought, flood, and heat. One value per place, shared by all three sectors.

S

Sensitivity

How much women's daily work and income depend on the resource under pressure in that sector.

A

Adaptive capacity

Access and assets that help women cope, such as safe water, modern energy, and land ownership.

Risk classes

Scores are grouped into five classes. The same thresholds apply to sector scores and composite scores.

ClassScore rangeReading
Very high75 to 100Severe hotspot. First in line for gender-responsive action.
High60 to 74.9Clear hotspot. Needs targeted programming.
Medium40 to 59.9Mixed picture. Check which component drives the score.
Low20 to 39.9Lower pressure. Monitor for change.
Very low0 to 19.9Least pressure among mapped places.

Sector indicators

These indicators feed the sensitivity and adaptive capacity components and are listed on every place's report.

SectorSensitivity draws onAdaptive capacity draws on
WaterShare of households where women collect water, and the time collection takesShare of households with access to safe water
EnergyShare of households depending on firewoodWomen's access to modern energy, and women employed in energy
AgricultureWomen's dependence on agriculture for incomeWomen's land ownership

For Kenya, the three components are taken as calculated in the sector workbooks. For the illustrative countries, water sensitivity weights the collection burden at 60% and collection time (capped at 60 minutes) at 40%, and land ownership is scaled against parity at an index of 0.5.

Limits to keep in mind

  • Scores rank places against a common scale. They are signals for where to look closer, not measurements of harm.
  • A single exposure value per place hides variation inside large counties, districts, and regions.
  • Indicators come from different years and sources, so small differences between places should not be over-read.
  • AI reports are drafted from the map metrics and general public context. Treat them as a starting draft and check them before use.
